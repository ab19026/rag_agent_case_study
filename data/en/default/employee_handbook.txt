## Part 2 – Onboarding Guide

### Chapter 4 – Check‑in and Onboarding Procedures

**1. Required Documents for Check‑in**  
- Original and copy of ID card  
- Original and copy of highest academic degree/diploma certificate  
- Resignation certificate (fresh graduates must provide a job assignment certificate)  
- Medical examination report (from a hospital designated by the company)  
- Personal bank card in the employee’s real name (for salary payment)  
- 4 bare‑head, white‑background, 1‑inch ID photos  

**2. Signing the Labor Contract**  
The company strictly abides by the *Labor Contract Law*; a written labor contract is signed on the day of onboarding. The standard contract term is three years.

**3. Probation Period Management**  
- **Duration:** For a three‑year contract, the probation period is 6 months for key management/actuarial roles, and 3 months for regular employees.  
- **Mentor System:** A senior employee is assigned as an “onboarding mentor” to help new hires acclimate.  
- **Confirmation of Regular Employment:** 15 days before the end of the probation period, the employee submits a *Probation Completion Report* for evaluation by the department head and HR. Upon approval, the employee becomes a regular staff member.

## Part 3 – Personnel Management System

### Chapter 5 – Working Hours and Attendance

**1. Working Hours**  
- **Standard working hour system:** Monday to Friday, 9:00–12:00, 13:30–18:00.  
- Special positions (e.g., claims adjusters, customer service hotline 955) may adopt a comprehensive working hour system upon approval.  

**2. Clock‑in Policy**  
All employees must clock in/out via DingTalk/WeChat Work using facial recognition and location verification. Failure to clock in without an approved leave request will be treated as half a day of absenteeism.

**3. Tardiness, Early Departure, and Absenteeism**  
- Tardiness/early departure exceeding 30 minutes but less than 4 hours = half day of absenteeism; exceeding 4 hours = one full day of absenteeism.  
- Three consecutive days of absenteeism or five cumulative days in a year constitutes a serious violation of company rules, and the company may terminate the labor contract without compensation.

### Chapter 6 – Leave and Time Off

**1. Types of Leave**  
- **Statutory Annual Leave:** 5 days for employees with ≥1 but <10 years of service; 10 days for ≥10 but <20 years; 15 days for ≥20 years.  
- **Additional Company Annual Leave:** On top of statutory leave, the company grants one extra day for each full year of service (capped at 5 days).  
- **Paid Sick Leave:** One full‑pay sick day per month (medical certificate from a community health center or above required).  
- **Marriage Leave:** 10 days upon legal marriage registration (3 statutory days + 7 local supplementary days).  
- **Other Leave:** Maternity/paternity leave, parental leave, and single‑child nursing leave follow local family planning regulations.  

**2. Leave Application Process**  
All leave requests must be submitted in advance via the OA system for sequential approval. In case of sudden illness that prevents advance request, the employee must notify their direct supervisor by telephone before 10:00 a.m. on the same day and submit the leave request within one working day after returning to work.

### Chapter 7 – Compensation and Benefits

**1. Salary Structure**  
- Monthly salary = Base salary + Position allowance + Variable performance base × Personal performance coefficient.  
- **Confidentiality:** Salary information is private and company confidential. Employees are strictly prohibited from inquiring about or disclosing each other’s salaries – violation will be treated as serious misconduct.  

**2. Payday**  
Salaries for the previous month are paid on the 10th of each month. If that day falls on a holiday, payment is moved to the nearest preceding business day.

**3. Social Insurance, Housing Fund, and Supplementary Benefits**  
- Social insurance and housing provident fund are contributed in full according to national regulations.  
- **Supplementary benefits (effective from date of hire):**  
  - Group accident insurance (coverage RMB 500,000)  
  - Group supplementary medical insurance (secondary reimbursement for outpatient/emergency and hospitalization; children may be added)  
  - Corporate annuity (eligible after completion of probation)  

**4. Annual Health Check‑up**  
The company provides a comprehensive annual health check‑up for every employee, with additional deep screenings (lung CT, tumor markers) for employees aged 35 and above.

### Chapter 8 – Performance Management

**1. Performance Cycles:** Quarterly review, semi‑annual evaluation, annual comprehensive assessment.  
**2. Performance Tools:** Balanced Scorecard (BSC) combined with Key Performance Indicators (KPIs), focusing on three dimensions: achievement of business targets, compliance quality, and customer satisfaction.  
**3. Application of Results:** Performance results are subject to forced distribution and determine monthly performance coefficient, annual bonus, annual salary adjustment, and promotion eligibility.

## Part 4 – Core Business Operation Standards

### Chapter 9 – Sales Conduct Compliance Management

**Red Lines (Violation leads to immediate termination of employment and potential legal action):**  

1. **No Sales Misrepresentation:**  
   - Do not offer benefits outside the insurance contract (e.g., rebates, expensive gifts).  
   - Do not make one‑sided comparisons between insurance products and bank wealth management products or deposit rates.  
   - Do not hype “product withdrawal” or “purchase restrictions,” nor falsely claim that a product will be fully withdrawn soon to induce purchase.  

2. **Audio‑Video Recording (Dual Recording) Standards:**  
   For any personal insurance new‑type product (participating, universal, investment‑linked) with a term exceeding one year, or for any large policy with annual premium exceeding RMB 1 million, strict “simultaneous audio‑video recording” (dual recording) is required. The recording must include identity presentation, explanation of policy terms, disclosure obligations, and risk warning statements.  

3. **Customer Information Authenticity:**  
   Employees are prohibited from forging customer signatures or risk disclosure statements, and from falsifying customer callback records. The phone numbers provided by the applicant/insured on the application form must be their own real‑name verified numbers.

### Chapter 10 – Underwriting and Policy Issuance

**1. Anti‑Money Laundering Customer Identification**  
If a single single‑premium policy reaches RMB 200,000 or the applicant/beneficiary has a sensitive nationality, the company must retain a scanned copy of the ID and conduct “beneficiary real‑name verification” and a “record of lawful source of funds inquiry.”

**2. Field Underwriting Investigation**  
For high‑sum‑assured cases (cumulative life insurance risk exposure exceeding RMB 3 million), an underwriting investigator must be dispatched to interview the insured, prepare a field investigation report, and verify consistency of occupation and financial status.

**3. Receipt and Callback**  
After policy delivery, the customer’s signature must be obtained within 3 working days, and 100% new‑policy callback must be completed during the free‑look period (15 days). During the callback, the company must confirm that the customer understands surrender losses, coverage period, and exclusions.

### Chapter 11 – Claims and Customer Service

**1. Service Turnaround Commitments**  
- Response within 10 minutes of receiving a claim notification, guiding the customer on required documents.  
- For medical claims with clear facts and an amount below RMB 5,000, settlement within 3 working days after complete documents are received.  
- For standard non‑investigation claims: determination within 5 working days.  

**2. Claim Denial Management**  
All denial decisions must be collectively reviewed by the Head Office Claims Committee. The *Notice of Claim Denial* must specify, line by line, the exact policy wording and general medical explanation supporting the denial. Verbal denial by phone is strictly prohibited.

**3. Complaint Handling Mechanism**  
- **First‑inquiry responsibility system:** The employee who first receives a complaint is responsible for follow‑up; shifting responsibility is prohibited.  
- Complaints forwarded by regulators (e.g., 12378 hotline) must be escalated to the provincial branch leadership for supervision, with customer contact and de‑escalation within 72 hours, and resolution with a formal opinion letter within 10 days.

## Part 5 – Information Security and Confidentiality

### Chapter 12 – Legal Obligations Regarding Customer Information Protection

The company strictly complies with the *Personal Information Protection Law* and the *Measures for the Administration of Customer Identification and the Keeping of Customer Identity and Transaction Records by Financial Institutions*.

1. **Minimum Necessary Principle:** Access customer policy details and medical records only when strictly necessary for business processing.  
2. **No Hoarding:** Prohibit copying customer lists (including ID numbers, phone numbers, health status) via USB drives, cloud uploads, or mobile phone photos and taking them outside the office network.  
3. **Data Masking:** In non‑essential display environments, customer names are shown as “Zhang San”, and ID numbers have asterisks in the middle digits.  
4. **Destruction of Information:** Paper waste containing customer information must be placed in confidential shredders, not mixed with ordinary trash.

### Chapter 13 – Network Security and Equipment Use

- **Internal/External Network Isolation:** Office computers are prohibited from connecting to private Wi‑Fi hotspots or unauthorized wireless routers.  
- **Email System:** Do not send attachments containing policy data using personal email accounts (QQ/163, etc.); use the company’s encrypted email system.  
- **Account Security:** Personal login credentials must be strictly protected. If another person (including a supervisor) uses your account to commit violations, the account holder bears full responsibility.

## Part 6 – Daily Conduct and Workplace Standards

### Chapter 14 – Professional Image and Etiquette

- **Dress Code:** Monday to Thursday are formal wear days (suit/professional attire). Friday is “Business Casual Day” – business polo shirts and casual trousers are permitted. Slippers, sleeveless tops, and ripped jeans are prohibited at all times.  
- **Telephone Etiquette:** Answer within three rings using the standard greeting: “Hello, this is [Your Name] from [Department Name], Ancheng Life. How may I help you?”  
- **Workplace Order:** Keep noise levels below 50 decibels in office areas. After lunch, use the designated break area for rest.

### Chapter 15 – Integrity and Conflict of Interest

**1. Anti‑Fraud:** Do not misappropriate premiums or fabricate claims to obtain payments. Any such conduct will be immediately reported to the police and referred to judicial authorities.  
**2. Gift Acceptance:** Do not solicit or accept kickbacks or valuable gifts from cooperating channels (hospitals, law firms, 4S shops, independent adjusters). Gifts valued over RMB 200 must be registered and turned over to the company.  
**3. Outside Employment:** Without company approval, employees may not act as shareholders, partners, or consultants for any other insurance company, intermediary, or investment/financial management company. Full‑time employees may not sell policies for competitors.

### Chapter 16 – Social Media and Self‑media Management

- Do not publish undisclosed claim cases, internal actuarial assumptions, or rate tables on public channels such as WeChat Moments or Douyin.  
- If you post as an insurance educator on social media, you must state “Personal views, not representing the company’s position” and refrain from criticizing competitors’ policy terms.  
- Do not use the company logo to operate private self‑media accounts.

## Part 8 – Disciplinary Actions and Separation

### Chapter 19 – Types of Disciplinary Actions

1. **Verbal Warning** (e.g., non‑willful tardiness, failure to wear proper attire)  
2. **Written Warning** (e.g., missing entries in logbooks, unauthorized release of non‑confidential data outside the company)  
3. **Demotion/Salary Reduction** (e.g., persistently lowest performance ranking, managerial negligence causing minor losses)  
4. **Termination of Labor Contract** (violation of red‑line prohibitions, criminal conduct, gross negligence causing significant loss or reputational risk)

### Chapter 20 – Separation Management

**1. Voluntary Resignation:**  
- During probation: 3 days’ notice.  
- After confirmation of regular employment: 30 days’ notice via the “E‑HR Resignation Module.”  

**2. Resignation Handover:**  
After approval, the following tasks must be completed before the last working day:  
- Return company assets (computer, ID badge, mouse/keyboard, business mobile phone).  
- Clear all advances and outstanding expense claims.  
- Sign the *Customer Information Confidentiality Undertaking* and *Non‑compete Agreement* (if applicable; the company will pay non‑compete compensation).  

**3. Secrecy Period:** Employees leaving key sensitive positions (e.g., IT, actuarial, product development) remain under an indefinite statutory confidentiality obligation.
